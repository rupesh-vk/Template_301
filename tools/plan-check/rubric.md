# Rubric: is this plan ready to post and build from?

## Checks

| Check | Evidence | Pass condition | Weight |
|---|---|---|---|
| Diagnosis follows from the evidence | The plan's stated cause, read against what the repro evidence actually shows (see `## Diagnosis and grounding` in `references/evidence-guide.md`). | The stated cause is consistent with every observation the repro evidence records, and the plan points at the specific observation that supports it. A cause that contradicts an observation in the evidence fails. A cause the evidence neither supports nor contradicts, offered without saying so, fails. A cause the plan marks as unconfirmed, with the step that would confirm it, passes. | required |
| Change is bounded | The plan's scope statement and its list of files to touch, read together against the diagnosis. | The plan states what it will change and what it will not, and everything in the change serves the stated cause. A plan that also refactors, renames, reformats, upgrades a dependency, or fixes a second defect found along the way fails, unless the plan shows that change is required to fix this one. A scope that cannot be read off the plan at all fails. | required |
| Fix targets the cause, not the symptom | The plan's approach, read against the cause the repro evidence points at. | The change acts at the place the evidence identifies as producing the wrong behaviour. A change that suppresses, catches, special-cases, or works around the observable symptom while leaving the identified cause in place fails. Where the plan deliberately chooses a narrower fix than the cause would warrant, it passes only if it says so and says why. | required |
| A stranger could start executing it | The plan's approach and its named files, read as instructions to someone who has the repro evidence and the repo but was not in the author's head. | A reader with the repo open could begin the first change without asking the author a question: the files are named, the behaviour to change is described in terms of what the code should do differently, and no step depends on a decision the plan leaves unmade. A plan that says what outcome it wants without saying where the work happens fails. | required |
| Test plan proves something observable | The plan's test plan, read against the repro evidence's own steps. | The test plan re-runs the behaviour the repro pinned down, and states what will be observed after the fix in terms someone could check — a value, an output, a test name and its result. A test plan that asserts the fix "works", "is verified", or "passes tests" without naming what is observed fails. A test plan that exercises a different path than the repro did fails unless it says why that path is the equivalent one. | required |
| Unknowns are stated as unknowns | The plan's risks and unknowns, read against the confidence of its other sections. | Anything the author has not confirmed is marked as unconfirmed where it is used, and the plan names at least one thing that could turn out otherwise, or states plainly that it found none and why. A plan whose approach rests on an assumption that appears nowhere in its risks fails. A risks section that lists only generic hazards unconnected to this change fails. | required |
| Comment fits the thread and the repo's conventions | The plan comment read against the issue thread highlights and the repo-facts block: its contribution policy, its stated conventions, any AI-use disclosure requirement, and whatever maintainers have already asked for on the thread (see `## Comms` in `references/evidence-guide.md`). | The comment answers what the thread has asked and meets every rule the repo states that applies to it, including an AI-assistance disclosure where the policy requires one. A comment that contradicts or ignores a maintainer's stated preference on the thread fails. A comment that restates the plan while leaving a direct question on the thread unanswered fails. A requirement the repo is silent on cannot fail here. | required |
| Comment is readable on its own | The plan comment read as a standalone thread post. | A maintainer reading only the comment learns the cause, the intended change, and how it will be verified, without opening another document. | preferred |

## Verdict rule

Ready (accept) if every `required` check grades pass. Any single `required` check grading fail makes the verdict hold (reject) — a required fail holds the plan regardless of how the others went.

An `unclear` grade on a required check counts as a fail. A plan is a promise made to a maintainer and to the author's future self, and a reader who has to guess which of two readings was meant will build the wrong one. The revision a hold asks for is almost always one sentence.

`preferred` checks never change the verdict. They are reported so the author can act on them before posting, and a plan may be ready with every preferred check failing.

The output quotes the evidence for the deciding check: on a hold, the first required check that failed and what the plan said instead; on a ready, the required check whose evidence was thinnest.
